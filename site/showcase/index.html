Production scenes.
Every light traced.

Two scenes the industry uses to test renderers, with the camera moving and all of their lighting path-traced. Then what the same engine does outside games, and small scenes you can open yourself.

Bistro night

Amazon Lumberyard Bistro · ORCA

A street corner after dark. The string lights, the shop signs and the lamps are the light sources: each one lights the square in its own colour.

  • Every emitter is a light
  • Warm light from the shop window and the wall lamp spills across the facade and the pavement

Drag the divider: three.js on the left, ThreeRT on the right, the same camera path.1

Zero-Day

Beeple · ORCA

A hall of machines lit by ten thousand small emitters. Each adds its own light, highlight and shadow, and the floor mirrors the machines and their lights.

  • Ten thousand small lights
  • Reflections of the whole scene, including the parts the camera never sees

The stills, side by side with three.js at its strongest: against three.js at full power.

Not only games.

Lighting of this quality used to take a studio, a render farm and a night of waiting. The same engine now runs a physics lesson, a jeweller’s counter, a flat you might buy and a museum hall, live, on the laptop or phone you already own. Each case below is one file of scene code: open it and try it.

Open on your device.

The hello-world scenes and a small game. Like the cases above, each is a package on this site: scene code at tutorial size and the files it reads. ThreeRT fetches it and traces it on your GPU. The same package opens as three.js in this browser.
